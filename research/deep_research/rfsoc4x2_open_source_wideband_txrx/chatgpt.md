# Open-source wideband TX/RX projects for the RFSoC 4x2

## Bottom line

As of **October 28, 2026**, I did not find another released, clearly open-source design that duplicates your exact combination of **2 TX + 4 RX, roughly 983 MS/s complex per channel, PL-DDR4-backed cyclic arbitrary TX, PL-DDR4 RX capture, PYNQ control, and MTS** on the Real Digital RFSoC 4x2. The closest projects separate into three architectural camps: **host-streaming SDRs** such as `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm` and `rfsoc4x2_uhd`; **local-memory/MTS designs** such as AMD/Xilinx `RFSoC-MTS`; and **very-high-rate network/offload designs** such as Strathclyde's QSFP project. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2 fileciteturn14file0L2-L2 citeturn25search0turn26search7

The two most important discoveries for your use case are relatively recent:

**`uceeyuf/rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm` is the strongest raw-data competitor/reuse source.** It is native to the 4x2 and demonstrates simultaneous continuous host↔RFDC streaming over one QSFP28 using RoCE v2/ERNIC: **64 Gb/s toward the DACs and 64 Gb/s from the ADCs at the same time**, with a 2 MB FPGA TX ring, 512 KB FPGA RX ring, 64 MB host RX ring, and MTS. Its raw transport/clocking/MTS code is open even though the optional "Modem 2" PHY is not. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2 fileciteturn17file0L2-L2

**`uceeyuf/rfsoc4x2_uhd` is the strongest software-ecosystem competitor.** It turns the RFSoC 4x2 into a UHD/RFNoC-style device with standard `multi_usrp`/MPM semantics, 100 GbE, DDC/DUC, RFDC NCO tuning and MTS. It has demonstrated 2 RX + 2 TX simultaneously at **500 MS/s per exposed channel using DPDK**, with no drops/overruns/underruns during the documented test. It gives up raw sample rate versus your design but gains the whole UHD application ecosystem. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2

Your present configuration is also interesting from a transport-budget perspective. At 16-bit I + 16-bit Q, your **4 × 983.04 MS/s RX** represents about **125.83 Gb/s of sample payload**, before framing; the corresponding **2 × 983.04 MS/s TX** is about **62.91 Gb/s**. Thus a single 100 GbE/QSFP28 link can plausibly carry your two TX streams, but not all four RX streams losslessly in that format. This is precisely where your use of **local PL DDR4 as a high-rate capture buffer** retains a fundamental advantage over one-port network-streaming designs.

The most reusable pieces I found for your existing implementation are therefore not necessarily complete alternative overlays:

* AMD/Xilinx **RFSoC-MTS** and `uceeyuf/rfsoc4x2_mts` contain the most directly relevant open MTS, SYSREF-aligned capture, URAM player/capture and residual inter-channel alignment work. citeturn25search0turn25search1 fileciteturn35file0L2-L2
* `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm` provides a serious alternative to DMA-through-PS for moving live sample data: hardware-driven RoCE rings and doorbells with essentially no processor involvement in the data path. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2
* Strathclyde's **100G QSFP offload** project provides a simpler direct RFDC→CMAC/UDP model and has demonstrated about **80 Gb/s** continuous RX offload. citeturn26search7turn26search8
* `rfsoc4x2_uhd` supplies something your architecture apparently does not: a reusable, standardized host API and RFNoC/UHD control plane. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2
* The various AWG projects contain compact cyclic-player RTL, but all of the released BRAM/URAM players are much shorter than what PL DDR4 gives you. fileciteturn18file0L2-L2 fileciteturn28file0L2-L2

“Every project” cannot be proven mathematically for public GitHub/GitLab and academic code, so I treated this as a best-effort exhaustive search. I included distinct projects and architectures I could identify, while collapsing pure forks and excluding repositories that only configure clocks/Linux without furnishing an RF TX/RX data path. A project marked **source-available / no explicit license** should not be treated as open-source for reuse even though its code can be read.

A few observations emerge from the native table.

First, **network streaming and deep local buffering solve different problems**. The RDMA design solves sustained transfer at 64 Gb/s each way exceptionally well, while your PL-DDR approach can capture bursts whose aggregate instantaneous rate exceeds the physical host link. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2

Second, the highest-rate open 4x2 DAC players overwhelmingly use **BRAM/URAM** rather than DDR4. That gives deterministic gap-free replay but restricts waveform duration: 131,072 samples in the selfoluap AWG is only ~13.3 µs at 9.8304 GS/s; the dedicated MTS design's training player uses only thousands of samples. fileciteturn22file0L2-L2 fileciteturn35file0L2-L2 Your DDR4-backed cyclic player remains unusual.

Third, **UHD is the only project I found that turns the board into something approaching a conventional off-the-shelf SDR from host software**, including standard channel objects, DDC/DUC, tuning, DPDK and RFNoC. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2

## Ranked shortlist for your design

**Best overall reuse target: `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm`.**  
This is the project I would inspect first at RTL level. Its strongest idea is the **hardware-host ring protocol**: the FPGA presents an RF stream directly to ERNIC, host RDMA writes maintain a TX producer pointer, and RX chunks autonomously issue RDMA writes to host memory. It has measured simultaneous 64-Gb/s TX and 64-Gb/s RX for 10 seconds with no sample-path errors in the documented run, while retaining MTS. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2

It **beats your design** wherever the requirement is indefinite acquisition/playback between the RFSoC and a powerful host: no finite PL-DDR capture depth, no PS copy path and no explicit stop/retrieve cycle. It is especially attractive if you want to augment your existing local-buffer mode with a “live stream” mode. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2

It **falls short of your design** for the exact 2TX/4RX ~983-MS/s-complex workload. Its proven continuous stream is 64 Gb/s in each direction, whereas your four 983.04-MS/s complex RX channels at 32 bits/sample total ~125.83 Gb/s. Your PL DDR4 can ingest that instantaneous aggregate during a finite capture even though a single external 100G link cannot. It also does not provide a large local DDR4 cyclic TX store. The optional high-level Modem 2 should not factor into reuse because its source is explicitly not published. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2 fileciteturn17file0L2-L2

**Best software/ecosystem target: `rfsoc4x2_uhd`.**  
This is the project to study if you want your RFSoC 4x2 to behave like a USRP-class device. It already supplies MPM/PetaLinux integration, UHD `mpmd`, RFNoC radio/DDC/DUC structure, 100 GbE, DPDK and standardized RFDC/NCO control. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2

It **beats your design** in software interoperability. Once a board appears through UHD, existing C++/Python UHD software and many GNU Radio workflows become accessible without writing a PYNQ-specific application protocol. It is also much better suited to truly continuous host streaming. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2

It **falls short in raw rate and buffering**. The strongest documented mode is 500 MS/s/channel for 2 RX + 2 TX, whereas your RX chains deliver about 983 MS/s complex each and you have four of them. UHD also gives you no equivalent to a large PL-DDR cyclic arbitrary-waveform store or deep local four-channel snapshot memory. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2

**Best synchronization and capture reference: AMD/Xilinx `RFSoC-MTS`, augmented with `rfsoc4x2_mts`.**  
AMD's project is the direct ancestor of several later 4x2 projects and remains the cleanest reference for the board's RFDC MTS plumbing, SYSREF and PL-DDR deep capture. The newer bare-metal `rfsoc4x2_mts` adds something valuable that standard MTS alone does not: it measures the residual end-to-end analog/converter skew with a wideband chirp and corrects it using RFDC coarse delay, reporting sub-picosecond residual correlation offsets in its loopback setup. citeturn25search0turn25search1 fileciteturn35file0L2-L2

These projects **beat your design only if you do not already perform post-MTS residual-path calibration**. Standard MTS establishes deterministic converter latency; the training-based scheme goes further by compensating the actual DAC+cable+ADC path difference. fileciteturn35file0L2-L2

They **fall far short on memory scale and host access**: the dedicated MTS project uses 64 k URAM captures and small URAM players, while the AMD design has only one explicit PL-DDR deep-capture channel in its demonstration topology. Your 2TX/4RX PL-DDR architecture is substantially more general. citeturn25search1 fileciteturn35file0L2-L2

**Best network-offload IP source: Strathclyde `rfsoc_qsfp_offload`.**  
The value here is its very direct RFDC→packetizer→100G-CMAC path. The project demonstrated around 80 Gb/s RX offload and ~2 GHz instantaneous bandwidth, making it one of the few public 4x2 projects that meaningfully exercises the QSFP28 at RF-scale throughput. citeturn26search7turn26search8

It **beats your design** if you currently retrieve PL-DDR captures through a slower PS path: integrating its CMAC/packetization architecture could make large post-capture drains—or decimated live monitoring—dramatically faster.

It **falls short** because the released design I could verify is fundamentally RX-centric. A later 2026 presentation describes a more complete bidirectional architecture with runtime DUC/DDC and MTS options, but I could not confirm that the corresponding TX implementation had landed in the publicly inspected repository branch. That claim is therefore **unverified for reusable public code**. citeturn29search8

**Best alternative DDR architecture to study: SPEAR+/SPEAR_BF, with a licensing warning.**  
Their custom StreamingDMA/DRAM-centric approach, multi-channel configurations and long waveform concepts are very close to the problem you have already solved. SPEAR_BF's published architecture goes as far as 2^31-sample waveform concepts, while SPEAR+ is expressly designed around real-time DRAM↔RF streaming. citeturn28search2turn28search4

They potentially **beat your design** in generalized multi-channel DMA abstraction and scaling to larger channel-count Gen-3 RFSoCs. They **fall short for immediate reuse** because their main hardware target is ZCU216, a real 4x2 hardware build was not established in this research, and I did not confirm an explicit open-source license. I would mine them for architecture, not copy RTL until licensing is resolved. fileciteturn32file0L2-L2

**Best compact TX block: `selfoluap/RFSoC4x2-AWG`.**  
If you need a small, comprehensible high-rate player to compare against your DDR4 TX engine, this is a good modern reference: dual DACs at 9.8304 GS/s, straightforward BRAM readers, PYNQ control, current Vivado 2024.1 build scripts, and recently restored DAC MTS/SYSREF support. fileciteturn18file0L2-L2 fileciteturn21file0L2-L2 fileciteturn22file0L2-L2

It **beats your design in simplicity and converter-rate playback**, but **falls short by orders of magnitude in waveform duration** and has no receive side. Its 131,072-sample per-DAC memory represents only about 13.3 µs at 9.8304 GS/s. fileciteturn23file4L64-L69

My practical ranking for your exact use case is therefore:

**`rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm` > `rfsoc4x2_uhd` > AMD `RFSoC-MTS` / `rfsoc4x2_mts` > `rfsoc_qsfp_offload` > SPEAR+/SPEAR_BF architecture > `RFSoC4x2-AWG`.**

QICK, CASPER and the Strathclyde modem/radio projects are good ecosystems or DSP references, but I would not replace your buffer architecture with them.

## What is actually reusable

The most promising improvement to your existing system is probably a **hybrid local-buffer + 100G streaming architecture**, rather than replacing it.

Your present PL DDR4 remains the right place to absorb the **125.83-Gb/s four-channel RX burst rate**. After or during acquisition, however, the data could be moved to the host through either the RDMA design's ERNIC machinery or a simpler CMAC/UDP pipeline. This would preserve your unique high-rate capture capability while eliminating a slower PS-mediated retrieval bottleneck. The RDMA implementation already solves ring management, hardware doorbells, chunk completion, host backpressure observability and restart behavior. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2

For TX, there are three genuinely different choices:

| Architecture | Best use | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Your PL-DDR cyclic player** | Long deterministic waveforms at rates too high for sustained host refill | Finite local memory; custom control software |
| **BRAM/URAM player** from RFSoC-MTS/AWG projects | Extremely deterministic short training sequences, chirps, pilots, calibration signals | µs-scale or tens-of-µs waveform duration at GS/s rates fileciteturn35file0L2-L2 fileciteturn23file4L64-L69 |
| **Host streaming** from RDMA/UHD | Unlimited-duration continuously changing signal | Bound by one 100G physical link and host/NIC behavior fileciteturn16file0L2-L2 fileciteturn14file0L2-L2 |

A particularly attractive design would expose **both** the PL-DDR player and live network streamer behind an AXI-Stream mux. Long precomputed waveforms could remain in DDR, while generated or adaptive samples could arrive over 100G. The `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm` TX ring even gives you a well-defined failure policy: an underfilled future sample position becomes zero while the timebase continues, rather than stalling the converter stream. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2

On synchronization, `rfsoc4x2_mts` contributes a useful distinction: **MTS is not the same as complete RF-path phase alignment**. Its measurements show deterministic tile latency after MTS but an additional one-sample path difference in some starts; the project then measures the real path with a broadband chirp and applies RFDC coarse delay. In the included I/Q OFDM test, that one-sample offset materially degraded 16-QAM until corrected. fileciteturn35file0L2-L2 If your own design currently trusts MTS alone, this is probably the highest-value synchronization feature to borrow.

UHD's contribution is almost orthogonal to the datapath. You could potentially preserve your PL-DDR engines while adopting selected ideas from `rfsoc4x2_uhd` for **clock programming, RFDC device abstraction, standardized channel numbering, NCO control and host discovery**. A complete RFNoC conversion would be more invasive, but the project proves that the 4x2 can be integrated into UHD's X4xx-style software model. fileciteturn14file0L2-L2

CASPER is most interesting if you intend to put **heavy DSP before storage**. Its reusable value is the mature FFT/channelizer/packetizer/100GbE ecosystem rather than its raw-recording model. For example, a four-channel full-rate acquisition quickly outruns a single external link, but FPGA channelization, sub-band selection or triggering can reduce the exported information rate. CASPER's 4x2 RFDC and 100GbE tutorials directly address that style of architecture. citeturn23search3turn23search4

QICK is similarly useful if you need **precise timed experiment sequencing** rather than raw bandwidth. Its tProcessor and waveform/readout abstraction solve deterministic timing extremely well, but its standard signal path deliberately transforms and decimates RFDC streams for quantum experiments; it is not a substitute for your 4×~983-MS/s deep recorder. citeturn24search0turn24search3

The SpectrumX project is valuable for another reason: it is a relatively clean example of turning the 4x2 into a remotely controllable field SDR, with service software, UDP packet metadata, clock selection, ADC NCO tuning and a reproducible Vivado build. Its 10GbE data plane is too slow for your target bandwidth, but the control-plane organization could be reused largely independently of the RF data path. citeturn23search0turn29search0

## Near-misses, closed pieces, and limitations

Several notable projects do not satisfy a strict definition of “open-source buildable TX/RX project,” despite being technically relevant.

**RadioStream** is probably the most important near-miss. The 2026 system is explicitly demonstrated on an XCZU48DR/RFSoC4x2-class prototype and uses 100GbE/DPDK for high-rate radio streaming, but the public GNU Radio repository says the FPGA bitstreams/Vivado projects and several other components are not contained there. The host code is open; the full RFSoC implementation was not reproducible from the public source set I found. citeturn26search0turn26search2

**Modem 2**, despite appearing prominently in `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm`, is closed. The important distinction is that the **raw RDMA streaming core, RFDC/MTS path and host code are open**, while Modem 2's transmitter/receiver RTL and models are explicitly not published. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2 fileciteturn17file0L2-L2

**SPEAR, SPEAR+ and SPEAR_BF** appear to publish substantial research code and hardware material, but I did not find a clear top-level open-source license in the checked repositories. Copyright law therefore does not give the same reuse rights as an MIT/BSD/GPL repository merely because the files are public. They are excellent architectural references, especially for DRAM↔RF streaming, but I have deliberately not presented them as legally clean drop-in OSS dependencies. citeturn23search1turn28search2turn28search4

The same caveat applies to **`lilyliu-nu/rfsoc-replay-buffer`**, which is technically one of the most directly relevant pieces of code I found: it explicitly implements load-once-from-DDR followed by CPU-free infinite BRAM replay on the RFSoC 4x2. The checked root tree had no LICENSE file. fileciteturn47file0L2-L2 fileciteturn48file0L2-L2

**NYU `mmwsdr`** is MIT at the visible top level, but I could not retrieve the FPGA repository/submodule needed to validate a buildable current hardware design. I therefore would not select it as a foundation until the missing FPGA source location is clarified. citeturn26search4

**MathWorks/HDL Coder RFSoC examples** are technically useful. In particular, ZCU208 examples demonstrate ADC→PL-DDR deep capture and PL-DDR→DAC waveform output and therefore map conceptually well to an XCZU48DR design. However, building those examples depends on commercial MATLAB/Simulink/HDL Coder/System Generator infrastructure, so I excluded them from the open-source shortlist in accordance with your scope. Their architecture is still worth comparing to your PL-DDR implementation. citeturn21search8turn5search5turn5search6turn5search7

**CASPER** sits in between: its libraries and tutorials are public, but a normal build uses MATLAB/Simulink and FPGA vendor tooling. I therefore included it because the FPGA/DSP source ecosystem is genuinely open and useful, while flagging its commercial build dependencies. citeturn23search3

I also found several repositories that I did not treat as independent TX/RX projects because they are supporting infrastructure or derivatives: RFSoC4x2 clock/PetaLinux bring-up projects, the board BSP, transport-only 100G/ERNIC/Corundum experiments, raw measurement datasets, and notebook-only applications layered directly on RFSoC-MTS. They can be useful components but do not constitute distinct arbitrary-TX + ADC-capture architectures. The `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm` project itself identifies its reusable ancestry explicitly: AMD RFSoC-MTS for player/capture/MTS, separate 4x2 ERNIC work for RDMA, and open Ethernet RTL. fileciteturn17file0L2-L2

The public-source picture therefore looks surprisingly clear:

**For local high-rate buffered acquisition, your design is already ahead of the public 4x2 baseline.** AMD's MTS project has PL-DDR deep capture but not your symmetric 4-RX/2-TX generic DDR architecture; the AWGs have excellent high-rate cyclic output but tiny on-chip memories; UHD and RDMA solve continuous host streaming but are constrained by the external link. citeturn25search0turn25search1 fileciteturn18file0L2-L2 fileciteturn14file0L2-L2 fileciteturn16file0L2-L2

**The strongest things you do not already have are probably a 100G live/fast-drain path, UHD compatibility, and post-MTS RF-path calibration.** Those come respectively from `rfsoc4x2_rdma_ofdm`/Strath QSFP, `rfsoc4x2_uhd`, and `rfsoc4x2_mts`. fileciteturn16file0L2-L2 citeturn26search7 fileciteturn14file0L2-L2 fileciteturn35file0L2-L2

The main unresolved items are the exact current public status of Strathclyde's newer **bidirectional** 100G design, a confirmed legally reusable license for the **SPEAR** family and the 4x2 replay-buffer project, and whether NYU's mmwsdr FPGA source has moved to a different public repository. Those are marked *unverified* rather than inferred from papers or presentations.
